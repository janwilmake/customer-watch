# My trust playbook

Read at Start and on every full tick. Facts about me are in `me.json`.

## The goal

What customer trust is for, for me: the company's relationships, and the ones that stay with me.

## My moves, in the order I prefer them

1. Tell the reporter myself, the day a fix goes live. Sign with my own name.
2. A short screen video instead of a long mail, for anything visual.
3. My booking link and phone number in every signature.
4. Connect on LinkedIn with every contact I deal with.
5. Coffee: invite them to the office at the end of a good call.
6. Help beyond the product, and say so when the product is not the right tool.
7. Ask for a referral after a value moment.

## Drafting

Every draft carries the one step from `nextstep.py`, worded in my voice.
- Shape: greeting → what they asked → what I did, with numbers → what is still open → the step → sign-off.
- Visit: "Fancy a coffee at our office some time? We are at <office>. Or I come to you."
- Meeting: "Shall we take 15 minutes to walk through <topic>? Pick a time with the button below."
- Referral: "Do you know another team that runs into this? I am happy to help them."
- Never ask while we owe them something late, or in a bad-news message.

## Calls

Notes for a proposed call: the goal, what we owe, the facts to tell, the one ask. Under 60 words.

## Boundaries

- The ledgers are my employer's data. Never export them. Delete them when I leave.
- Check my contract for a non-solicitation clause.
- A public post uses counts from `trust.py` without `--names`.
